\section{Temperature-dependent properties and prescribed sensitivity}
\label{sup:temperatureproperties}
The nominal pore and oil laws, source definitions and applicability
assumptions are stated with main Eqs.~\ref*{M-eq:porediffusivitytemperature}
and~\ref*{M-eq:oilheatcapacity}--\ref*{M-eq:drymatrixsensibleenergy}.
The pore coefficient is evaluated at each common face temperature and
pressure. Retained-water mobility, retention, conductivity, geometry and
baths remain fixed; no moisture target or outlet is refitted.

\paragraph{Reference-magnitude sensitivity.}
The reference magnitudes $2$, $4$ and $6\times10^{-10}$ m$^2$/s are
prescribed alternatives, not a measured uncertainty interval. Each case
starts from the original feed; the DT regime-A feed is common because
binary pore transport is inactive there. The three DT histories were
computed with the code of the convergence ladder (Sec.~\ref{sup:ladder}),
including the retained-water continuation, at 16 radial cells and the
middle time level; the nominal case is the ladder's own 16-cell history.
Each passes the independent water, hexane and energy reconstruction from
the original feed to exit at $10^{-10}$, with largest normalized defects
of at most $7.3\times10^{-12}$. The earlier four-cell Faner histories below
pass the independently accumulated exterior-rate audit at
$4\times10^{-10}$.

The Faner falling interval and outlet change very little across these cases. The adaptive histories use different accepted intervals and documented recovery controls. The subsecond differences in core-ending time are smaller than the 2.3-s change in that time between 4 and 64 cells in the convergence ladder (Sec.~\ref{sup:ladder}), so at this resolution they cannot be separated from numerical-history effects; the falling interval, 17.7~s, is the same at every resolution of the ladder.

\paragraph{Temperature-shape comparison.}
In the earlier two-cell calculation, at the same reference magnitude 4 and with the oil law unchanged, a complete constant-$D_b$ DT history gives 138.286 ppm wet hexane and 17.4648\% moisture. Applying the Fuller shape changes them by +0.075\% and -0.00291 percentage points. The reference magnitude therefore matters more here than this prescribed temperature shape.

The temperature-shape hexane difference is smaller than the observed regime-C timestep change below; it should not be interpreted as a resolved physical improvement.

\paragraph{Regime-C timestep check.}
With the same accepted regime-B checkpoint, the nominal laws, factor 100 and $K_\ell=0.32$ mol/(m$^2$ s), reducing the maximum regime-C step from 5 to 2.5 s changes wet-basis moisture by +0.01170 percentage points, residual hexane by -0.295\%, temperature by -0.00063 K and first recorded film depletion by -1.079 s. Both histories pass the independent component and energy audit at $10^{-10}$. This checked regime-C timestep sensitivity of the former two-cell history; full-history mesh and timestep convergence was established later by the ladder of Sec.~\ref{sup:ladder}.

\paragraph{Oil heat capacity and caloric consistency.}
The oil fit and its analytic energy integral are given in main
Eqs.~\ref*{M-eq:oilheatcapacity}--\ref*{M-eq:drymatrixsensibleenergy}.
At 105$^\circ$C it gives 2158.45 rather than 2000 J/(kg K).
The reference anchoring cancels the oil correction for Faner; at the DT oil
fraction 0.013740458, dry-matrix capacity at 105$^\circ$C changes from
2318.8621 to 2317.9313 J/(kg K), or $-0.04014\%$. This is a capacity
comparison, not a bound on the nonlinear trajectory error. Regenerated
DT feed reaches shell activation 2.46 microseconds earlier than the
former constant-oil feed. Its independent exterior-rate audit is stated
in Sec.~\ref{sup:dtmarch}. The physical property adapter supplies the same capacity to wet and dry
temperature derivatives; stored constant capacity fields remain numerical
reference scales.

Numerical recoveries and accepted-state controls for these cases are
collected in Sec.~\ref{sup:numericalcontrols:properties}. Earlier contact,
mobility-factor and mesh comparisons retain their constant-property scope.
The new laws remain conditional material assumptions, not validated binary
transport or an equipment model.
\subsection{Targeted refinement of the former two-cell DT history}
\label{sup:currentDTrefinement}
This subsection records the targeted four-cell refinement of the former
two-cell DT history, made before the retained-water continuation. Its open
resolution question is answered by the uniform-mesh convergence ladder of
Sec.~\ref{sup:ladder}, whose 64-cell history the main text now reports.
The targeted refinement retains the adopted oil $c_p(T)$, pore $D_b(T,P)$,
total liquid-connected mobility factor 100, $K_\ell=0.32$ mol/(m$^2$ s),
feed and prescribed bath. The uniform accepted regime-A endpoint is imported
on four radial cells without changing its inventories. Regimes B and C
are then continued to the original 1766.5355-s outlet. Regime A itself is
not refined. Table~\ref{tab:sub:currentDTrefinement} compares this accepted
continuation with the former two-cell history and its regime-C timestep
check.

The four-cell history is a composite continuation with ordinary regime-B
step requests 0.1, 0.05 and 0.025 s; geometric events and positive-core
targets have their own solved durations. Its folder's final-step label
does not describe a uniform timestep over the complete trajectory.
The two-cell controller's single-face scheduling was generalized to every
grid face using the same approach ratio $0.18/0.125=1.44$; the native
arrival and departure solves are unchanged. Positive-core target halving
is restricted to the central cell so it cannot skip a refined-grid face.
The departure duration is bounded by the requested ordinary step rather
than remaining fixed at 0.05 s. These are controller changes for the
refinement, not changes to transport laws or acceptance tolerances.

An initial root refusal, an attempted target crossing a grid face, and
departure attempts failing the combined ledger are retained. At the last
face a 0.05-s departure gave normalized hexane defect
$1.335\times10^{-10}$ and was refused; a 0.025-s departure passed the
unchanged $10^{-10}$ gate. The first smaller ordinary-step request still
used the hard-coded departure duration, so its identical failure is not
independent timestep evidence. The final 663 accepted advances pass the
independent reconstruction from activation to outlet: maximum normalized
water, hexane and energy defects are $1.71\times10^{-11}$,
$2.26\times10^{-12}$ and $9.17\times10^{-13}$. Core extinction is at
502.923 s and water-film depletion at 685.735 s.

Relative to the former two-cell history, moisture increased by 0.402 percentage
points and residual hexane decreased by 15.67 ppm (11.3\%). The corresponding
two-cell diffusivity-range spans were 0.296 percentage points and 15.21 ppm.
Thus resolution sensitivity was not negligible relative to the reported
physical sensitivity. This two-resolution comparison changes both the grid
and regime-B controls; it gives neither an observed continuum order nor
an extrapolated error bound. Separate spatial and temporal refinement,
further grid levels and refinement of the Faner history were therefore
needed; the uniform-mesh ladder of Sec.~\ref{sup:ladder} supplies them
and shows that the two-cell DT outlet differed from 64 cells by 12.0\% in
hexane and 0.66 percentage points in moisture. The two-cell numbers in
this subsection are kept as the record of that earlier calculation, not
as results of the paper.
