\noindent\textbf{Purpose and scope.} This Supplementary Material explains the parameter sources, balance formulation, comparison inputs and numerical checks behind the particle calculations. The numerical evidence combines operator verification, conservation checks and selected mesh and time-step sensitivities; complete-trajectory refinement remains the next step in assessing convergence.

The two pure-hexane comparisons examine different transport descriptions: the 2008 falling-rate curves use a \emph{separate Fickian shell}, while the 2019 soybean run uses the water-bearing radial particle. Its volume-mean temperature is compared with the local holder thermocouple as a distinct observable. The DT experiment follows the particle through a prescribed steam-rich bath and uses published outlet ranges as industrial context. Experimental assessment of coupled water and hexane removal requires paired component histories; application to an operating DT also requires tray-scale gas and energy balances and independent process observations. The source release matched to these results is described in Sec.~\ref{sup:deposit}; a preservation archive with a persistent identifier is being prepared.

\noindent\textbf{Reading conventions.} All solvent and water loadings are kilograms per kilogram of dry, water- and solvent-free composite meal. A \emph{declared} input is a chosen, bounded model assumption; a \emph{measured} input comes from a material or process observation. An \emph{identified} coefficient is evaluated against the same series used to obtain it, whereas an independent prediction uses separate observations. Alternative readings of an unspecified mass basis or size-distribution shape are reported separately. The thermocouple measures a local thin-layer temperature, whose position within the particles is unknown. Numbers prefixed S refer to this document; numbers without the prefix refer to the main manuscript.

The equation tables in Sec.~\ref{sup:equations} collect the balances,
supporting closures and source references. They distinguish the solved
particle equations from coefficient constructions, diagnostic limits and
the separate historical Fickian comparison.

\section{Process, balances and independent property inputs}
\label{sup:derivations}\label{sup:ale}\label{sup:closures}
The regional inventories, fluxes and surface assumptions are defined in
main Sec.~\ref*{M-sec:model}. This section derives the moving-front energy
jump and binding calorics, then records the precise property inputs and
film convention. Component storage includes every local phase; evaporation
and retention redistribute it without creating a component source.

The exact balances over a moving control volume are
Eqs.~(\ref*{M-eq:movingcomp}) and~(\ref*{M-eq:movingenergy}) of the main
text. Here $u$ is the total internal energy density, $j_k$ a molar component
flux and $\JE=q+\sum_k\bar h_kj_k$ the total energy flux, on the same datum
as storage. Terms proportional to the boundary speeds are fluxes of swept
material; dropping them creates or destroys material, even when a
fixed-mesh solver converges. The jump conditions,
Eqs.~(\ref*{M-eq:rhcomp}) and~(\ref*{M-eq:rhenergy}), are the thin-volume
limits of these balances. The wet-side \emph{donor} is the material actually
swept, not an equilibrium interface trace substituted into only one balance.

The denominator of the front velocity, Eq.~(\ref*{M-eq:frontvelocity}), is this jump written so that the latent heat appears once. Eq.~(\ref*{M-eq:shellconserved}) removes the wet-side relative hexane flux, so the dry-side hexane flux is $\dot{\front}(C_{\mathrm{h,d}}^{\Gamma}-C_{\mathrm{h,w}}^{\mathrm{b}})$. Substituting it into the energy jump leaves
\begin{equation}
\bar{h}_{\mathrm{h,d}}\bigl(C_{\mathrm{h,d}}^{\Gamma}-C_{\mathrm{h,w}}^{\mathrm{b}}\bigr)
-\bigl(u_{\mathrm{d}}^{\Gamma}-u_{\mathrm{w}}^{\mathrm{b}}\bigr)
\label{eq:sub:latent}
\end{equation}
in the denominator. On one caloric datum, $h_{v}-u_{l}=\Delta h_{\mathrm{vap}}+(h_{l}-u_{l})$. For the liquid, $h_{l}-u_{l}=Pv_{l}$ is negligible compared with the latent heat. For an ideal vapour, $h_{v}-u_{v}=RT/M_{\mathrm{h}}$, so $u_{v}-u_{l}=\Delta h_{\mathrm{vap}}-RT/M_{\mathrm{h}}$. The volumetric denominator therefore contains the latent heat of the liquid hexane swept by the front, a smaller ideal-gas correction on the pore-gas trace, and the binding-potential difference already stored in $u$. The smooth-region balances have no separate vaporization source, so this heat is not counted twice.

\subsection{A shared donor, retained phases and caloric closure}
\label{sup:donor}\label{sup:binding}\label{sup:stefanrecovery}\label{sup:singlecomponent}
The historical core loading and algebraic total-flux closure are defined
in main Sec.~\ref*{M-sec:model:local}; their heat-fed front limit is
explained in main Sec.~\ref*{M-sec:model:front}. The separate Fickian shell
of Eq.~\eqref{eq:ficklimit} instead assumes a slow transport path that
the binary total-flux closure does not supply.

At a moving interface, the water and energy donor terms both use the state of the consumed wet-core material. A necessary discrete datum test is $\partial R_E/\partial c_{\mathrm w}=R_{\mathrm w}$, with $R_E$ and $R_{\mathrm w}$ the interfacial energy and water residuals and $c_{\mathrm w}$ an arbitrary shift of the caloric zero of water. When a stored bulk water term is mixed with a swept equilibrium trace, the test exposes a hidden pre-drainage assumption. The resolved moving-front comparisons in the main text use the corrected donor; both prescribed-bath experiments use the same radial closure and conservative transitions between regimes. The binding energy of retained hexane comes from the same Guggenheim--Anderson--de Boer (GAB) activity law that sets the stored solvent. With sorption potential $\psi_{\mathrm h}=(RT/M_{\mathrm h})\int_0^W\ln a_{\mathrm h}(\xi,T)\,\dd\xi$, the caloric potential is $\Bh=T\partial_T\psi_{\mathrm h}-\psi_{\mathrm h}$. No separately fitted heat-of-sorption source enters the energy equation. The modified Luikov water isotherm, Eq.~(\ref*{M-eq:luikov}), gives the retained-water activity within its measured activity and temperature domain. Condensing water on a surface exposed to vapour and retaining water in the meal are different operations: condensation changes the material inventory, and retention defines a constitutive state function.

\subsection{Parameters, source hierarchy and film definition}
\label{sup:parameters}\label{sup:luikov}\label{sup:oilmap}\label{sup:virial}\label{sup:filmconvention}
This subsection records the source conventions behind the adopted parameter
set, Table~\ref*{M-tab:parameters} of the main manuscript, which separates
published property fits, derived quantities and declared transport inputs.
The 2008 thesis and 2019 article are each used for their own runs, and a
printed table takes precedence over a digitized plot. The 2019 samples' mean
sphericity is not claimed as measured for the 2008 samples. Each published
correlation has its own definition of gas velocity, voidage and transfer
area; borrowing a coefficient without them would obscure the comparison.

The cross second virial coefficient of the binary gas uses the
corresponding-states correlation of \citet{plyasunov2003crossvirial}, with
central interaction $k_{\mathrm{wh}}=0.494$ and source interval
0.477--0.511. Both completed main trajectories use the central value;
listing the interval does not mean that the full coupled histories were
recalculated at its endpoints. The upper water capacity is the modified
Luikov value at source-domain activity 0.799, $0.235670904$ kg/kg dry; the
main table shows it rounded to 0.236. The reference lower moisture anchor is
0.0230 kg/kg dry, and the adopted positive-water continuation has no lower
moisture floor. It retains $A_1=0.880$, $A_2=12.184$ and the analytic log
activity of main Eq.~\ref*{M-eq:luikov}; exactly absent water is a separate
limit. Tests confirm bitwise identity within the original measured band.
The reference retained-water mobility is
$8.4741855104\times10^{-7}$ mol/(m s), constructed in
Sec.~\ref{sup:watermobility} and held fixed in the pore study.
The reference meal heat capacity is 2317 J/(kg K), shown as
$2.32\times10^3$ in the main table; these display choices do not alter
the calculation inputs.
The derived dry-meal density used in the calculation is 1159.65 kg/m$^3$;
it is shown as $1.16\times10^3$ kg/m$^3$ in the main table.

The bed-film correlation is
\[
\Nu_\varepsilon=0.6941\Rey_\varepsilon^{0.579}\Pra^{1/3}.
\]
\citet{faner2008desolventizado} prints 0.6941; the value 0.6949 transcribed by
\citet{coletto2022modeling} is not used.

The bed groups are $\Rey_\varepsilon=\Rey/(1-\varepsilon)$ and
$\Nu_\varepsilon=\Nu\varepsilon/(1-\varepsilon)$, with holder voidage
$\varepsilon$ and approach-velocity Reynolds number based on equivalent
diameter. Whitaker's separate packed-bed correlation \citep{whitaker1972forced}
is evaluated at each 2008 rig state; the duty scores under both mass
conventions are in Sec.~\ref{sup:filmmatch}. The 2019 boundary instead
uses the constant-rate exposure construction of Sec.~\ref{sup:fanermultiplier}.
The oil anchoring and fluid calorics follow main
Sec.~\ref*{M-sec:thermo:properties}; the older additive oil-arm floor
remains a separate sensitivity bracket (Sec.~\ref{sup:evidence:outlet}).

\section{Conservative numerical method and observed accuracy}
\label{sup:activation}\label{sup:acceptance}\label{sup:verification}
This section supports the main text's numerical method and verification. The finite-volume scheme uses one swept wet-volume rate, Eq.~(\ref*{M-eq:sweptvolume}), for the wet cells, interface and dry cells. The front cuts a fixed set of material shells; grid-motion fluxes at the faces cancel in pairs when inventories are summed. Backward-Euler stepping solves the regional storage changes and the interface's component and energy jumps as one nonlinear system. The event at which the external liquid disappears is found from mass and energy conservation together, with event time and temperature as unknowns. An exactly dry core loses its degrees of freedom instead of being carried as an infinitesimal artificial core. Each event is checked with its corresponding interface equations. State acceptance requires positive phases, a front that does not grow and non-negative holdups; an infeasible trial leaves the previous state unchanged.

Figure~\ref{fig:sup:particlesolver} separates time advancement from nonlinear
iteration. Physical closures are first evaluated at the initial guess to
prepare the residual and its scaling. At each subsequent trial state,
state-dependent properties, phase equilibrium and transport fluxes are
re-evaluated before forming the coupled water, hexane and energy residuals.
Surface-water and retention-capacity states follow the selected equations;
liquid-water imbibition requires a film-connected dry shell. Conservative
event transfers are included in the balances checked before accepting the
state. Numerical recovery follows the declared controls of the relevant stage.

The manufactured-solution tests measure the convergence order of \emph{one operator} at a time. The complete particle histories require their own refinement study. Every accepted step closes the water, n-hexane and energy balances, per step and cumulatively. This schedule probes the response to abrupt boundary changes and differs from the physical histories of the laboratory and DT experiments. The manufactured-solution and balance rows above it use separate operator and conservation tests.

\subsection{State acceptance and numerical refinement}
\label{sup:moved:verification}\label{sup:moved:ladder}
For the numerical reference tests, the scaled nonlinear residual and physically normalized conservation bounds are $10^{-10}$ per step and cumulatively. The activation subsystem keeps its separately declared $2.5\times10^{-10}$. At an exact grid face, the departure tangent admits an inward root under a declared $1.0\times10^{-10}$ bound, never an outward-moving front. When a feasible state or event cannot be found under these equations and balance criteria, the calculation retains the input state and reports the failure. The refinement ladder uses radial counts $12,24,48,96$ and steps $0.075,0.0375,0.01875$ seconds on the same \SI{0.225}{\second} stress schedule. Adjacent differences must decrease and the finest pair must agree within one percent (\SI{0.1}{\kelvin} in temperature). Front position and inventories pass this test on that schedule (Table~\ref{tab:sub:verification}).

The 2008 dry-shell curves have a different numerical test: the \emph{surface-read} variable-coefficient march (Sec.~\ref{sup:surfaceloading}) is second order in shell mesh (observed $2.00$--$2.12$) and first order in step ($1.10$--$1.16$); the reported residuals are within one percentage point of their continuum extrapolations. The current water-bearing experiments continue on a stationary radial particle after a conservative projection removes the core. Their history-specific checks (Secs.~\ref{sup:journalmarch} and~\ref{sup:finitecontact}) quantify selected mesh and time-step sensitivities. Component and energy conservation are verified at each accepted step; establishing spatial and temporal convergence requires refinement of the complete histories.

\section{Experimental sources and the regime comparison}
\label{sup:validationdetail}\label{sup:datasetversions}\label{sup:tworegimes}\label{sup:filmmatch}\label{sup:moved:prereg}
The 2008 thesis \citep{faner2008desolventizado} and later article
\citep{faner2019kinetics} supply distinct traces for the regime comparison.
Their source conventions and the 2019 setup are collected in
Sec.~\ref{sup:fanerinputs}; the versioned scored points and redistribution
scope are described in Sec.~\ref{sup:deposit}. These pure-hexane records
assess solvent removal, while coupled water uptake requires steam-exposed
observations.

The constant-rate period tests the external heat supply: the coefficient is evaluated at each run's reported gas state, holder footprint and sample mass. Reading the printed mass as the wet charge, the ratio of calculated to measured duty is $0.82$--$1.06$, and that of crossing times $0.86$--$1.13$. Reading the same mass as dry meal gives $0.53$--$0.61$ of the duty and $1.45$--$1.70$ of the crossing time. The thesis's printed flux convention supports the wet-charge reading but does not give an independently measured film coefficient. Of its three bed correlations, Whitaker's gives the closest coefficient. The bracketing criterion set before the comparison is not met, because the coefficients inferred at two conditions lie $3.7$ and \SI{4.2}{\percent} below Whitaker's. The departure is retained in the duty comparison. This benchmark examines the external correlation and the supplied $\Xc$; identifying a hexane diffusion coefficient requires a separate intraparticle transport measurement. The measured boiling-point plateaux separately check the pure-hexane property equation.

The regime test is applied before any transport model is chosen. For each of the four plotted traces: interpolate the time at which the measured loading crosses $\Xc=\SI{0.20}{\kilogram\per\kilogram}$; average the thermocouple over the first thirty seconds; and interpolate the first rise one kelvin above that average. A plateau that ends after the crossing, at a temperature consistent with solvent boiling, is consistent with a receding liquid-hexane core. A plateau that ends before it, with the temperature several reading half-widths above boiling, is inconsistent with a boiling core at that loading. The diagnostic is the sign, not the precise heat-limited time. Table~\ref{tab:sub:criterion} keeps each trace's own clock and shows that the sign is unchanged for rise thresholds of one to five kelvin. Distinguishing the intraparticle mechanisms requires spatial temperature measurements and controlled run conditions beyond those supplied by the source.

\section{The 2008 falling-rate curves and independent uptake}
\label{sup:surfaceloading}\label{sup:item27}\label{sup:item29}\label{sup:item31}\label{sup:item32}\label{sup:moved:uptake}
The scalar Fick description used in the main text's separate comparison with these older, slower drying records is
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\!\left[\varepsilon_{\mathrm{g}} c_{\mathrm{h,g}}
 + \Cdm W_{\mathrm{eq}}\right]
 = \frac{1}{r^{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}
   \!\left[r^{2}\Deff\,\frac{\partial c_{\mathrm{h,g}}}{\partial r}\right],
\label{eq:ficklimit}
\end{equation}
where $\varepsilon_{\mathrm g}$ is the gas-filled porosity,
$c_{\mathrm{h,g}}$ the hexane mass concentration in the pore gas, kg/m$^3$,
$W_{\mathrm{eq}}$ the local equilibrium retained hexane, kg/kg dry meal,
and $\Deff$ an effective transport coefficient
\citep{cardarelli2002modeling}. The equilibrium capacity belongs inside the
storage derivative, not inside the divergence as a face mobility.
This equation serves only this separate comparison; neither its diffusivity
nor its scalar flux replaces the binary particle law in the two main
experiments.

The 2008 thesis meal is already hotter than boiling at the reported critical loading. Its falling-rate trajectories are therefore tested against the \emph{separate} Fickian shell of Eq.~\eqref{eq:ficklimit}, not against an unobserved front. The soybean-meal apparent diffusivities of \citet{cardarelli1998thesis} refer to total stored solvent and need a storage conversion before they enter a pore-gas gradient model; the retained-hexane isotherm of \citet{cardarelli1996hexane} supplies it. The law used here is regressed on the seventeen soybean coefficients, so applying it to the 2008 sunflower trace is a cross-meal check, not a same-meal prediction. At \SI{50}{\celsius} twelve uptake series identify coefficients $8.4$--$20.1$ times the tabulated apparent values, within the model's storage retardation at that temperature. Since those values came from the same uptake curves, the match is an identification, not a new prediction. At hotter process conditions the retardation approaches unity, so the multiplier inferred from the low-temperature curves cannot be transferred unchanged.

The source's thirty-four uptake coefficients across \SIrange{50}{95}{\celsius}
define the separate effective law
\begin{equation}
\ln\Deff=\ln A-\frac{E_{\mathrm a}}{R_{\mathrm g}T}
+\beta\ln(\Xh/X_{\mathrm{ref}}),\label{eq:sup:defflaw}
\end{equation}
with $A=\SI{3.513e-9}{\meter\squared\per\second}$,
$E_{\mathrm a}=0.31\pm\SI{16.1}{\kilo\joule\per\mole}$,
$\beta=0.959\pm0.331$ (95-percent half-widths), and
$X_{\mathrm{ref}}=\SI{0.01}{\kilogram\per\kilogram}$. The residual standard
deviation is $0.410$ in $\ln\Deff$. The law is extrapolated
\SI{41}{\kelvin} to the thesis drying
conditions and read at the \emph{surface boundary loading} once per step, a
declared convention. A weighted-mean diffusivity \citep{crank1956diffusion}
predicts much faster desorption, and local evaluation gave no mesh-refined
result on the tested spheres. Numerical convergence of the surface-read
convention does not extend to those other implementations or remove the
temperature and geometry uncertainty. Table~\ref{tab:sub:dry} reports
root-mean-square (RMS) errors as percentages of the measured loading drop
and counts points within the digitization band. Both particle sizes use the
same coefficient law without refitting; geometry alternatives and the
coefficient's prediction band are reported separately
(Table~\ref{tab:detail:geometry}).

The declared 2008 volume-to-surface radii are 0.7215 mm for sunflower and
0.666 mm for soybean. Their shape-transfer assumption, its selection
after radius screening and the separate geometry sensitivity are
specified in Sec.~\ref{sup:detail:spheres}.

In the surface-read model the curves dry \emph{more slowly} than in either 2008 measurement. The residuals on the declared spheres and the range of faster and slower traces within the prediction band show the remaining discrepancy. Interpreting the radius and diffusivity sensitivities physically requires independent particle-shape and transport measurements. The comparisons support examining a sorption-storage-corrected Fickian path in the earlier samples; resolving their microstructure, process-temperature transport coefficient and differences from the later Faner source requires further measurements. A two-size experiment on one feed, with loading and particle temperature both measured, would help distinguish film-dominated from shell-dominated scaling.

\section{The Faner experiment with a water-bearing particle}
\label{sup:journalmarch}\label{sup:journalensemble}
This section reports the Faner history and its supporting sensitivities.
The complete experimental inputs and observable definitions are collected
in Sec.~\ref{sup:fanerinputs}; the exposure construction is in
Sec.~\ref{sup:fanermultiplier}. Constitutive assumptions are specified with
the main equations, and numerical controls in Sec.~\ref{sup:numericalcontrols}.
The nominal four-cell calculation retains the experimental clock and
the constant-rate boundary calibration. Its event times and outlet
values are reported in main Sec.~\ref*{M-sec:validation:faner} and
Table~\ref*{M-tab:journalmarch}. Water and core volume are shown in
Fig.~\ref{fig:fanersupport}. The maximum Ackermann argument is
\FanerMaxBeta, within the unit reference band and the declared $|\beta|\le3$
study band. The loading discrepancy remains visible; no falling-rate or
temperature point is fitted.

\paragraph{Earlier mesh and timestep sensitivity.}
\label{sup:fanerrefinement}
The following comparisons retain the earlier constant pore diffusivity
and oil capacity. They do not establish convergence of the updated
temperature-property history.

For the earlier constant-property, smaller-radius case, halving the four-cell step of the
stationary dry particle from 2.5 to 1.25 s changed final hexane by 0.24\%,
water by 0.19\% and mean temperature by 0.072 K. These earlier time-step
checks concern the 0.808-mm sphere. The earlier constant-property source-mean sphere was
compared at two and four cells; because the positive-core step sequences
differ, the whole difference cannot be assigned to the mesh alone. For this
radius, the two-cell and four-cell core-depletion times differ by 2.0 s; at
240 s, hexane differs by 0.024\%, water by 1.3\% and temperature by
0.031 K. These comparisons quantify numerical sensitivity at the stated
resolutions; complete-history refinement is needed to establish convergence.

The initial water loading was varied over 0.025, 0.05 and 0.10
kg/kg dry meal; regime-A histories are admissible at all three. The new
0.05 attempts at the upper corner and at low water mobility find no
admissible leading pore profile by shooting, and the 0.10 leading jump seed
at the upper corner does not converge. These bounded searches identify continuation difficulties at the tested
inputs. A converged continuation is needed before the corresponding
physical response, including any phase change, can be assessed. Robustness to initial moisture and the regime-A
water-access assumption remain open. The successful 0.025 charge is not
presented as a validated reconstruction of a source experiment whose
moisture was not reported.


\subsection{Size distribution and startup interpretation}
\label{sup:fanersizes}
The source size descriptors and nominal sphere are defined in
Sec.~\ref{sup:fanerinputs}. The size and startup sensitivities below retain
the earlier constant $D_b=6\times10^{-10}$ m$^2$/s and oil capacity
2000 J/(kg K).

An earlier construction used a lognormal number
distribution truncated to 0.5--4 mm, with log-width about 0.280 and
constraints from the source mean and 1--3-mm fraction. Weighting by $d^3$
gives mass quantiles of about 1.50, 2.15 and 3.03 mm at 10, 50 and 90\%.
The main calculation uses the source mean diameter as a volume-equivalent
sphere. A new coarse size sensitivity uses three bins of equal dry mass
with mass-mean diameters 1.60, 2.15 and 2.89 mm, using the earlier constant
$D_b=6\times10^{-10}$ m$^2$/s. With the surface-to-volume
mapping $R=\psi d/2$, the corresponding radii are 0.593, 0.796 and
1.068 mm. Their positive-core marches approach core depletion at
about 49, 76 and 118 s. The spread of core-depletion times illustrates how a size distribution
broadens removal. Completing the class continuations through core extinction
is needed to construct a charge-weighted curve and assess the effect over
the full falling-rate interval.
The assumed distribution is constrained by source summaries, not by fitting
the measured drying trajectory.

For classes of equal density and oil content, number weights must be
converted to dry-mass weights before superposition:
\[
q_c=\frac{p_c d_c^3}{\sum_b p_b d_b^3},\qquad
\overline X_k(t)=\sum_c q_c X_{k,c}(t).
\]
The mean temperature of such an ensemble still differs from a local
thermocouple reading. Choosing a distribution to obtain a desired curve
shift would not identify the experimental size distribution.

The startup sensitivities below keep the earlier 0.808-mm sphere.
The unresolved preheat endpoint is described in Sec.~\ref{sup:fanerinputs}.

A bounded
regime-A calculation with the same particle, bath and exposure gives
external-hexane depletion at 41.078 s for a start at the boiling
temperature, 41.437 s at an initial 68.5$^\circ$C and 42.143 s at
68.0$^\circ$C. This modest subcooling thus shifts that event by about
0.36--1.07 s; it does not produce a 10--20-s shift of the temperature curve.
These calculations cover regime A; a complete drying history would
also require continuation through regimes B and C.
Starts at 65 and 60$^\circ$C gave no admissible step in the bounded search
of the evaporation-only regime-A equations. A colder start therefore needs a
separately examined thermal and phase treatment of the surface before it
can give a new full curve; it is not represented by shifting the existing
trajectory in time.

\subsection{Source-size and vapour-temperature sensitivities}
\label{sup:fanerassumptions}
This subsection reports complete two-cell sensitivity calculations with the
same transport coefficients, initial loadings and experimental clock.
Replacing the earlier 0.808-mm radius with the source-mean
0.885-mm radius moves the 0.20-kg/kg crossing from 41.0 to 46.6 s and
lengthens the 0.20--0.10-kg/kg interval from 15.5 to 17.7 s. At 240 s,
the temperature falls from 116.0 to 114.3 degrees Celsius. The size change
thus improves two observables through a physical change in area per dry
mass and transport distance; the remaining discrepancies stay explicit.

A second calculation keeps the source-mean state at its computed core
depletion, 90.1 s, and then applies the measured vapour-temperature trace
to the same stationary-particle equations. The digitized gas readings are
joined linearly; the last value, 117.82 degrees
Celsius at 235.058 s, is held to 240 s. The final particle temperature is
113.0 degrees Celsius and hexane is 0.00168 kg/kg dry, compared with
114.3 degrees Celsius and 0.00161 kg/kg in the constant nominal bath.
This calculation isolates the response to the measured temperature
trace after core depletion; the earlier history retains the nominal bath. Its independent whole-history
hexane, water and energy defects remain within $4\times10^{-10}$.

More water could delay warming through evaporation and heat capacity, but
it also changes the phase equilibrium and coupled transport. The water
loading of the source charge is not reported; it was dried under nitrogen
and stored in air before being re-soaked in hexane. Raising the water only to lower the temperature would need
information the experiment does not provide.
\par
If the denominator is truly dry meal, reading total mass loss as solvent
loss gives an apparent loading $X_{\mathrm h}(t)+X_{\mathrm w}(t)
-X_{\mathrm w}(0)$. Water loss lowers this quantity, so it does not
explain the underpredicted late solvent loading. Whether the source used a
dry or an as-is denominator is a further uncertainty of interpretation.
\par
A further shell-formation search at 0.050 kg/kg, with central differences
and the default finite-volume seed, was stopped when it exceeded its
allotted search time; it gives no additional drying trajectory.

In short, the calculated particle dries faster than the measured charge in
the falling-rate period.

\section{Process interpretation, sensitivity and limits}
\label{sup:missingfull}\label{sup:extrapolationflags}\label{sup:regime}\label{sup:regimegrid}\label{sup:desolventizerfloor}\label{sup:envelopescoring}\label{sup:identifying}
This section connects the particle results to DT operation. The particle supplies the component and energy source or sink terms needed by a tray model. Coupling those terms to tray-scale gas and meal histories requires accounting for incoming particle loadings and sizes, steam condensation, residence, gas composition, mixing, jacket heat and vapour escape. Transfer of a thin-holder film coefficient to an industrial bed requires the corresponding gas flow, contact area and heat path; the source leaves the local sample heat path and probe position unspecified.

Within its modeled range, at the constant gas state of each separate march,
the moving-front formulation is heat-fed: the binary pore mobility separates
water from n-hexane but adds no independent resistance to their total
escaping flux. For the \emph{separate Fickian dry-shell comparison}, the
ratio of conductive heat supply, expressed as an evaporation flux, to
diffusive solvent supply is
\begin{equation}
\Lambda=\frac{k_{\mathrm{dry}}(T_{\mathrm g}-T_{\mathrm{int}})
                  /\Delta h_{\mathrm{vap}}(T_{\mathrm{int}})}
                {D_{\mathrm{eff}}[c_{\mathrm{sat}}(T_{\mathrm g})-c_{\mathrm s}]},
\label{eq:sub:bottleneck}
\end{equation}
where $c_{\mathrm{sat}}$ and $c_{\mathrm s}$ are the n-hexane mass
concentrations at saturation and at the particle surface, respectively.
On the declared gas-state grid, $\Lambda$ ranges from $7.80\times10^3$ to
$6.29\times10^4$ over states with a receding front. A value above unity
means that this Fickian comparison is limited by its assumed solvent
transport. The ratio characterizes the separate Fickian description at fixed gas
states. Assessing the controlling resistance in steam-wet meal requires
the coupled heat and component responses along a drying history.

Table~\ref{tab:sub:scope} summarizes what each comparison establishes and
the measurements needed to distinguish the remaining mechanisms (DTDC:
desolventizer-toaster-dryer-cooler).

With pressure and source-water-isotherm alternatives propagated, the ideal retained-water vapour-pressure diagnostic $a_{\mathrm w}(W_{\mathrm w})P_{\mathrm{sat}}^{\mathrm w}(T^*)=P$ gives $100.5$--$107.6$ degrees Celsius, a range spanning a published band of industrial discharge temperatures. The calculation assigns the supplied moisture to the retained-water inventory; resolving bound, interstitial and external water separately requires phase-partition measurements. The single point at the moisture of the temperature source itself lies 2.05 kelvin above that band. This cross-source, water-only equilibrium calculation describes retained-water activity. Boiling of a separate free-water droplet depends on that droplet's own liquid state, whereas the coupled particle temperature follows the transient component and energy balances. The binary particle calculation uses water-component fugacity and calculates its temperature independently of $T^*$. At declared last-tray hexane mole fractions $0.003$--$0.03$ and temperatures 100--110 degrees Celsius, a dry-particle equilibrium calculation gives $4.5$--$66.0$ ppm without and $8.1$--$112.4$ ppm with a residual-oil solution arm (Sec.~\ref{sup:evidence:outlet}); only the richest and coolest oil-arm corner reaches the published residual-hexane class. These values describe the equilibrium inventory at the supplied conditions; reaching it depends on the transient transport history. The total-meal GAB isotherm already includes reference oil, so assessing the oil-arm overlap requires independent mixture data.

Source-domain limits and constitutive extensions are stated with the main
property equations (Sec.~\ref*{M-sec:thermo:properties}); the front and
imbibition assumptions are in main Secs.~\ref*{M-sec:model:front}
and~\ref*{M-sec:model:liquidcontact}. The formulation does not identify
shrinkage, a distributed phase change or re-wetting of a receded core.
Three groups of transport and surface coefficients have positive intervals
without source-backed endpoints. Numerical conservation does not resolve
these material uncertainties.

The measurements in Table~\ref{tab:sub:scope} can be organized as a
two-size pure-hexane series with temperature through the crossing,
same-meal transport and retention measurements at process temperature,
and paired solvent--water histories under a known steam supply. Holder
geometry, exposed area and probe wetness must accompany those observations.
Equipment assessment additionally needs independent feed, gas, steam,
wall-duty, residence and discharge records, followed by the dryer-cooler
and whole-tower residence distribution.

\section{Reproduction inventory and accessibility}
\label{sup:deposit}
The release matching this submission is at
\url{https://github.com/tomas3svoboda/dt_particle_model}, submission snapshot
\texttt{submission-2026-10-15}, with a SHA-256 manifest of every file. It holds
the particle-model source code; the scripts that produced the two particle
histories and the sensitivity calculations of Sec.~\ref{sup:finitecontact};
their run records, including the saved states from which each segment
starts and the numerical-recovery records; the prescribed DT bath as a
declared input file; the digitized readings of the 2019 article; the
scripts that rebuild the three computed figures (two in the main text, one
in this supplement) and check the printed numbers against their records;
and the LaTeX sources and PDFs of both documents. Files keep their relative paths, and the release's
README gives the commands. The equipment
model that computed the prescribed DT bath is not part of the release, so
the bath cannot be regenerated from it. Some early steps of the Faner
calculation and the $K_\ell=0.10$ case were run with earlier revisions of
the calculation scripts; each step record stores the hashes of the
scripts it used. The release holds the records behind the figures and the
headline numbers of the main text, and the earlier records that the number
check reads; the number check binds the printed numbers of both documents
to the released records and names those that rest on records the release
does not hold. Such records, among them the authors' working notes, the
derivation records and the independent balance record of the DT feed
history, are available from the corresponding author on request.
Other digitized readings are not in the release; they are shared where the
publishers' terms permit. The authors' code
is under MIT terms, which do not cover the third-party publications or
their digitizations. A preservation archive with a persistent identifier
is being prepared.
