Dear Editor,

Please consider **A particle model of hexane removal and water uptake in
soybean meal desolventizing**, by Tomáš Svoboda, Marcela Sluková, Tomáš Moucha
and Phuong Hoang, as an Original Article in the *Journal of Food Process
Engineering*. Supplementary Material and a graphical abstract accompany the
manuscript.

Steam desolventization couples solvent removal, water uptake and heating in
porous oilseed meal. Our contribution is a conservative particle formulation
that follows these processes through external liquid hexane, a receding
liquid-hexane core and the subsequent radial drying tail. Component and
energy jump conditions determine interface motion, while a separate exterior
water inventory exchanges water with the meal through finite liquid contact.
Temperature-dependent oil heat capacity and effective pore diffusivity are
included, with their literature basis and remaining assumptions stated.

The same formulation is exercised against two prescribed gas baths. For the
120 °C pure-hexane experiment of Faner et al. (2019), the calculated interval
from 0.20 to 0.10 kg hexane/kg dry meal is 17.7 s, compared with 25.4 s
measured. In a prescribed desolventizer-toaster history lasting 1766.5 s,
the 1.5-mm-radius sphere exits with 17.5% wet-basis moisture, 138 ppm
wet-basis hexane and a mean temperature of 105.0 °C. The moisture and solvent
values fall within published industrial outlet ranges. Independently
accumulated water, hexane and energy balances close within 4 × 10⁻¹⁰ in
normalized units through the complete histories.

These comparisons establish feasibility under specified conditions; they do
not establish predictive validation. The paper retains the Faner timing
discrepancy, distinguishes industrial ranges from paired measurements and
reports parameter and numerical sensitivities. The liquid-water mobility is
anchored to related soybean-meal uptake evidence through an explicit
diffusion-timescale estimate, rather than fitted to the outlet. The model
provides component and energy fluxes for future coupling to equipment
balances, which we believe makes it relevant to the journal's food-process
modeling and separation readership. No new physical experiment is reported.

The source code, released run records, figure and number checks, and LaTeX
sources are available at https://github.com/tomas3svoboda/dt_particle_model
(submission-2026-10-15 snapshot, with a SHA-256 manifest). A preservation
archive with a persistent identifier is being prepared.

The manuscript is original, has not been published and is not under
consideration elsewhere. The manuscript states that no specific grant
supported the research. It discloses Tomáš Svoboda's employment by Siemens,
s.r.o., which had no role in the study design, analysis or decision to
publish; the other authors declare no competing interests. The manuscript
also declares AI assistance for language editing, code review and manuscript
organization, with the authors responsible for all scientific content.

Thank you for considering our manuscript.
