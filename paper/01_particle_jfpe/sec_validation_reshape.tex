\section{Results}\label{sec:validation}
This section applies the particle model to two prescribed baths. The
Faner experiment compares solvent loading and temperature with source
readings; the DT experiment follows the coupled solvent, moisture and
thermal response of a particle from feed to exit. Both use the same
particle conservation laws, phase equilibrium, retention closures and
reference transport coefficients (Table~\ref{tab:parameters}), without
refitting; their gas histories, geometry and initial states differ.

\subsection{Falling-rate drying in Faner's superheated-hexane experiment}
\label{sec:validation:journalmarch}\label{sec:validation:fanermarch}
\label{sec:validation:faner}\label{sec:validation:hexanebp}\label{sec:results:lumpedlaw}
\paragraph{Experimental basis and declared particle.}
The soybean run of \citet{faner2019kinetics} supplies pure n-hexane vapour
at \SI{120}{\celsius} and atmospheric pressure. We represent its reported
mean diameter by a sphere of radius \SI{0.885}{\milli\metre}, initially at
the calculated hexane boiling temperature, \SI{68.8}{\celsius}.
Initial loadings are $X_{\mathrm h}=0.475$ and $X_{\mathrm w}=0.0250$
kg/kg dry meal, with oil mass fraction 0.0195. The source reports
preheating but neither its exact endpoint nor initial moisture; these are
declared initial conditions. The sphere preserves equivalent volume, not
flake shape or a mass-weighted size distribution. Source preparation,
size descriptors, alternative mappings and measurement definitions are
collected in Sec.~\ref*{S-sup:fanerinputs}.

\paragraph{Heat supply and shared model.}
The exposure multiplier 0.146 carries a boundary calibration from the
source's early constant-rate duty, using an earlier size ensemble
(Sec.~\ref*{S-sup:fanermultiplier}). It scales heat and mass coefficients
at fixed spherical area, giving $h_Q=15.1$ rather than 104 W/(m$^2$ K).
It was not reidentified for the present sphere. The heat duty does not
independently identify mass transfer, so applying the same multiplier to
both coefficients is a boundary assumption. Falling-rate and temperature
readings are reserved for comparison, conditional on that boundary.

The separate 2008 constant-rate duty checks, including their alternative
sample-mass conventions and departures from Whitaker's correlation, are
reported in Sec.~\ref*{S-sup:filmmatch}.
An otherwise unchanged calculation with multiplier one exhausts external
free hexane at 6.8 s, compared with 46.8 s in the reported calculation.
This early-stage sensitivity demonstrates the importance of the gas-side
boundary; it does not establish that the industrial reference coefficients
represent Faner's apparatus (Sec.~\ref*{S-sup:detail:geometry}).
For reference, Faner's geometric comparator is
\begin{equation}
\front=\Rp\left(\frac{\Xh-\Xe}{\Xc-\Xe}\right)^{1/3},
\label{eq:faneroracle}
\end{equation}
Here $\Xe$ is Faner's equilibrium hexane loading for the source's gas
conditions. In contrast, $\Xe^{*}(T,P)$ in Section~\ref{sec:physical:regimes}
is our retained-hexane reference evaluated at the pressure-feasible
saturated activity $a^{*}$. Both have units kg hexane/kg dry meal, but
they refer to different equilibrium specifications and are not
interchangeable. Neither fixes the core-extinction loading in the present
calculation, which solves the component and energy jumps.

The regime-A water-access assumption is
stated in Section~\ref{sec:model:waterfilm}; water evolves through both
transport paths after shell formation. Imbibition is inactive in this
experiment because no external water film forms.

\paragraph{Solvent removal and temperature.}
External free hexane disappears at \SI{\FanerFreeEnd}{\second}; the core vanishes at
\SI{\FanerDryTime}{\second}. Loading crosses 0.20 kg/kg at
\SI{\FanerCrossTwenty}{\second} and reaches 0.10 kg/kg a further
\SI{\FanerFallingInterval}{\second} later, versus 45.9 s and 25.4 s in
the digitized source. The calculated falling interval is about
\FanerFallingShortfall\% shorter.
At 240 s, hexane is $\FanerExitHexane$ kg/kg dry, water is
$\FanerExitWater$ kg/kg dry and mean temperature is
\SI{\FanerExitTemperature}{\celsius}.
The computed interval is the same at every mesh and time step of the
convergence ladder (Section~\ref{sec:verification:convergence}), so the
discrepancy lies in comparing one declared sphere with a measured charge;
Section~\ref{sec:discussion} attributes it to the size dispersity and
non-uniform drying of the holder layer. The particle sizes are known only
as summaries, the initial moisture is unknown, and the local exposure and
the thermocouple sampling are uncertain.
Table~\ref{tab:journalmarch} summarizes the comparison; loading crossings
are interpolated between successive calculated states.

\paragraph{Regime-C hexane transport in the pure-hexane bath.}
Decomposing the calculated hexane flux at the outer dry-shell face into
total vapour motion and binary diffusion, as in Eq.~\eqref{eq:retflux},
shows that hexane is carried outward by the moving pore gas. Binary
diffusion contributes in the opposite direction in all 310 recorded
regime-C advances, offsetting 0.005--0.68\% of the net outward hexane flux.
These contributions describe how hexane moves in this calculation;
they do not by themselves identify the rate-limiting resistance.
Their reconstruction is given in Sec.~\ref*{S-sup:fluxcontributions}.

In earlier constant-property calculations ($D_b=6\times10^{-10}$ m$^2$/s,
oil capacity 2000 J/(kg K)), adopting the source mean diameter delayed the
80--100$^\circ$C milestones by 12--17 s at four cells relative to the
inherited smaller sphere, improving the loading crossing while leaving a
falling-rate discrepancy (Sec.~\ref*{S-sup:fanerassumptions}). The
two- and four-cell marches of that calculation lost their cores at 90.1
and 88.1 s. In the present convergence ladder the core disappears at 87.4, 86.1, 85.5, 85.2 and 85.2~s at 4, 8,
16, 32 and 64 cells, so the earlier spread was a mesh effect that the
refinement removes; the falling interval stays at 17.7~s throughout.

The measured vapour varies from 117.8 to \SI{120.7}{\celsius}. An earlier
constant-property two-cell sensitivity that applies this trace after core
depletion gives \SI{113.0}{\celsius} at 240 s, compared with
\SI{114.3}{\celsius} under constant vapour temperature
(Sec.~\ref*{S-sup:fanerassumptions}). The small improvement supports treating
heat-supply variation as an experimental uncertainty; it does not close the
full temperature discrepancy.
Startup calculations at higher initial moisture, 0.050 and 0.100 kg/kg,
are not yet resolved (Sec.~\ref*{S-sup:journalmarch}).

\subsection{Particle history in the prescribed DT bath}
\label{sec:validation:dtmarch}\label{sec:validation:otherchecks}
\label{sec:validation:uptake}\label{sec:validation:tail}\label{sec:validation:bp}
\label{sec:validation:envelope}\label{sec:validation:rewetting}\label{sec:validation:derived}
\paragraph{Bath and initial state.}
The DT experiment uses an effective spherical radius $\Rp=\SI{1.5}{\milli\metre}$
and follows the particle from feed to the prescribed exit at
\SI{1766.5}{\second} (29.4 min). This radius is consistent with the upper end of the
0.5--1.5-mm equivalent-radius range containing 95.1\% of Faner's soybean
particles \citep{faner2019kinetics}, but is not a measured DT mean.
The longer heat path and smaller surface area per unit mass make this a
useful coarse-particle case when examining residence needed for solvent
removal. It does not represent a mass-weighted meal size distribution.

Initial temperature is \SI{49.0}{\celsius}, hexane and water loadings are
0.388 and 0.124 kg/kg dry meal, and oil mass fraction is 0.0137.
The feed is cooler than the 57--60$^\circ$C examples reported by
\citet{witte_nd_soybeanmeal} and \citet{kemper2013solvent}, so sensible
heating remains part of this experiment. The selected temperature is a
scenario input, rather than a measurement for the simulated charge.
The pre-desolventizing (PD) bath is at \SI{72.0}{\celsius} with hexane mole
fraction 0.664, exposure fraction 0.00408 and indirect heat supply
85 W/kg dry until \SI{490.2}{\second}. Steam contact then leads to a
toasting bath ending at \SI{105.0}{\celsius}. The prescribed gas history
is detailed in Sec.~\ref*{S-sup:dtmarch}.

The sequence represents indirect pre-desolventizing followed by live-steam
contact, as described by \citet{kemper2013solvent}. Indirect heating removes
solvent before steam contact and reduces the solvent load on the
steam-heated trays. The selected exposure fraction approximates the small
exposed area of a bed compared with the summed areas of isolated spheres.
This exposure fraction, the imposed duty and the bath ladder together
define the experiment; they do not identify a measured industrial
operating point.
Liquid-water imbibition uses the contact and mobility estimates of
Section~\ref{sec:model:liquidcontact}.

\paragraph{Liquid depletion, condensation and imbibition.}
At PD exit, about \DTPDFreeHexane\ kg external free hexane/kg dry remains. It disappears
approximately 2.1 s later while the external water film accumulates.
The initial two-liquid surface equilibrium is near \SI{61.3}{\celsius};
it differs from pure hexane's boiling point. The dry shell forms at
\DTDryShell~s, as the external free hexane is exhausted, and core
extinction follows at \DTCoreExtinction~s, \DTShellToExtinction~s later. Retained hexane and pore gas then
continue to evolve in regime C. Both the rapid surface evaporation and the
core recession follow from the coupled balances.

At core extinction, the particle holds \DTExtinctionWater\ kg water/kg dry
and the external water film a further \DTExtinctionFilm\ kg/kg dry. With
retained water admitted above the Luikov anchor
(Section~\ref{sec:thermo:properties}, Eq.~\eqref{eq:waterbridge}), imbibition draws the film into the
particle quickly. The film is gone at \DTFilmDepletion~s, about nine
minutes after feed and one minute after dry-shell formation, well before
the long regime-C stage that sets the outlet hexane; it persists into the
first 50~s of regime C. Liquid-water imbibition and evaporation back to the
bath compete for this inventory; condensation does not imply that all
incoming water remains in the particle.

\paragraph{Regime-C hexane transport in the steam-rich bath.}
The two gas-phase contributions change within regime C. At the first
recorded advance, 505.1 s, outward total vapour motion carries 77.6\%
of the net outward hexane flux at the outer dry-shell face and binary
diffusion 22.4\%. While the particle takes up water, from 512.6~s to the
water peak at 1385.1~s, the total gas flux is inward, and hexane leaves
only because binary diffusion carries it outward against the incoming gas.
After the peak the particle begins to dry, the total gas flux turns
outward, and in the \DTCells-cell calculation binary diffusion then opposes
hexane removal: at exit it amounts to $-132$\% of the net flux, against
$+232$\% from gas motion. Diffusion therefore sets hexane removal through
the water-uptake period, and outward drying flow carries it afterwards
(Sec.~\ref*{S-sup:fluxcontributions}). The split itself is not
mesh-converged. The net exit flux is small beside its two opposing parts,
and the diffusion share at exit is $+95$, $+50$, $-36$, $-87$ and
$-132$\% at 2, 8, 16, 32 and 64 cells, changing sign between 8 and 16
cells; the gas-motion share at the first advance likewise falls from
98.5\% at 2 cells to 77.6\% at 64. These shares describe the calculation
on its finest mesh, not a resolved partition of the transport.

\paragraph{Exit observables and numerical sensitivity.}
Particle water at exit is \DTExitWater\ kg/kg dry meal, equivalent to \DTFiniteMoisture\%
wet-basis moisture, inside the 16--22\% range published for DT outlets. Hexane is \DTFiniteHexaneDry\ mg/kg dry, or \DTFiniteHexaneWet\ ppm on a wet basis, and
volume-mean particle temperature is \SI{\DTFiniteTemperature}{\celsius}.
The external water film is depleted. The particle-only basis conversion
uses $1+X_{\mathrm w}+X_{\mathrm h}$; water in the external water film is reported
separately while the film is present.
Table~\ref{tab:dtmarch} compares the result with the published outlet
ranges. Calculated ppm uses wet meal; the source solvent range does not
specify its analytical mass basis. The calculation uses \DTCells\ radial
cells and a 2.5-s maximum regime-C timestep, the finest uniform mesh of the
convergence ladder at its middle time level
(Section~\ref{sec:verification:convergence}).

The calculated moisture and hexane lie within the published ranges, and
the volume-mean temperature sits 0.3~K below the \SI{105}{\celsius} lower edge. These ranges are industrial context, not a validation: the agreement cannot identify the
imbibition coefficients or validate the prescribed bath.
The reference pore diffusivity $D_{b,\mathrm{ref}}$ of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:porediffusivitytemperature} is an assumed value, so its
effect on the outlet was computed with the same code at 16 radial cells
and the middle time level. Varying it from $2$ through the nominal $4$ to
$6\times10^{-10}$ m$^2$/s gives exit moisture of 18.58, 18.82 and 18.99\%
wet basis, residual hexane of 128.3, 125.4 and 122.8 ppm wet basis and
temperatures of 104.85, 104.80 and \SI{104.78}{\celsius}; all three
histories pass the independent whole-history audit. The moisture span,
0.41 percentage points, is larger than the estimated 64-cell numerical
error of outlet moisture, 0.19 points, and about half the effect of
$W_{\mathrm{sat}}$; the hexane changes by $+2.3$\% and $-2.1$\% of the
nominal value, far less than under $W_{\mathrm{sat}}$. This interval is a
prescribed sensitivity range; it does not measure uncertainty in the
material coefficient (Sec.~\ref*{S-sup:temperatureproperties}).

Section~\ref{sec:verification:convergence} shows the 64-cell outlet values
of Table~\ref{tab:dtmarch} converged in time, and in the mesh for hexane and
temperature. The outlet
moisture still changes by 0.066 percentage points between 32 and 64 cells,
at an observed order of about one half, under a thin diffusion layer at the
film-free surface. That residual is far smaller than the 1.0-point shift
produced by the retained-water continuation of Eq.~\eqref{eq:waterbridge}
itself: at 32 cells the outlet moisture is 18.9\% with the continuation and
17.9\% with retained water capped at $\Wcap$.

In the independent whole-history audit of Section~\ref{sec:numerics},
whose DT defects are at most $\DTAuditWater$ against $10^{-10}$, the feed
history up to dry-shell activation stays below $1.6\times10^{-12}$.
History-specific numerical controls and recoveries are collected in
Sec.~\ref*{S-sup:numericalcontrols}.
