\section{Gas-composition and outlet-equilibrium calculation}
\label{sup:evidence:outlet}\label{sup:evidence:checks}\label{sup:evidence:outstanding}
This section gives the equilibrium calculation behind the residual-hexane floor quoted in the main text. The pressure-feasible solvent activity used for the outlet check is $a_{\mathrm{h}}^{*}=\min(1,\yh P/P_{\mathrm{sat}}^{\mathrm{h}}(T))$. The \emph{equilibrium loading} is evaluated at a specified $y_h$ and temperature; the time needed to approach it follows from the transient transport calculation. Table~\ref{tab:evidence:floor} separates GAB-retained solvent from the total dry-shell inventory, which also holds pore gas; its grid samples a literature-based range of last-tray conditions. The last-tray total of $4.5$--$66.0$ ppm without the oil-solution arm refers to $y_h=0.003$--$0.03$ and 100--\SI{110}{\celsius}. With the oil-dissolution sensitivity it becomes $8.1$--$112.4$ ppm; this band must not be formed by adding the arm to a total-meal isotherm as if their overlap had been measured. In the experiments' pure-hexane gas the floor is many times higher. The DT march of Sec.~\ref{sup:dtmarch} reads this floor at its declared exit gas, $y_h=0.01$, below the dry-shell tail.

Two numerical checks examine the limiting behaviour of the coupled formulation. In the ideal-binary reduction the scalar Fick identity holds to $6.34\times10^{-16}$, while at finite pressure the low-water coefficient comparison leaves a \SI{3.799}{\percent} discrepancy retained in the comparison. The default coupled interface formulation at \SI{409.15}{\kelvin} admits a pore hexane fraction of at most $0.857$; this restriction does not define the supplied external bath. The Faner experiment with a water-bearing particle treats the pure bulk gas separately from its binary state in the pores and at the surface; the earlier property-table checks are separate from this march. \subsection{Gas-phase flux contributions after core extinction}
\label{sup:fluxcontributions}
The transport decomposition uses the outer dry-shell face of the same
Faner and DT histories plotted in the main text. For a recorded hexane
transfer $\Delta n_{\mathrm{h,out}}$ over a step $\Delta t$, the particle-side
boundary ledger gives $N_{\mathrm h}^{\mathrm g}
=\Delta n_{\mathrm{h,out}}/(4\pi\Rp^2\Delta t)$. The recorded gas flux
$\Nt$ excludes retained-matrix water transport. The discrete advective
composition is the upstream value $y_{\mathrm{h,up}}$: the outer-cell
value for outward gas motion and the surface value for inward motion.
The contributions are
\[
N_{\mathrm h}^{\mathrm g}=y_{\mathrm{h,up}}\Nt+J_{\mathrm h},
\qquad
f_{\mathrm{motion}}=\frac{y_{\mathrm{h,up}}\Nt}{N_{\mathrm h}^{\mathrm g}},
\qquad
f_{\mathrm{diffusion}}=\frac{J_{\mathrm h}}{N_{\mathrm h}^{\mathrm g}}.
\]
These are signed fractions of net outward hexane flux. Negative values
oppose removal; a fraction greater than unity represents an outward
contribution partly cancelled by the other contribution.
Table~\ref{tab:sub:fluxcontributions} gives representative values.

Binary diffusion opposes outward hexane removal in all 310 recorded
Faner regime-C advances, by 0.005 to 0.68\% of the net flux. In the
64-cell DT history, total gas motion turns inward at 512.6~s, while the
external water film is still being imbibed, and stays inward through the
water-uptake period until the particle water peaks, at
\SI{0.237}{\kilogram\per\kilogram} dry, at 1385.1~s; hexane then leaves only
by outward binary diffusion against the incoming gas. From the peak to exit
the particle dries, the total gas flux is outward and diffusion opposes
removal. These shares are not mesh-converged: the net exit flux is small
beside its two opposing parts, and the diffusion share at exit is $+95$,
$+50$, $-36$, $-87$ and $-132$\% at 2, 8, 16, 32 and 64 cells, while the
gas-motion share at the first advance falls from 98.5\% at 2 cells to
77.6\% at 64 cells. The reported clocks are discrete observation times, not
localized events marking zero total gas flux. The decomposition identifies
the direction and relative magnitude of the two gas-phase contributions.
Determining which resistance controls removal requires a separate sensitivity
analysis of heat supply, pore diffusivity and retained-water transport.
