\begin{abstract}
In a desolventizer-toaster (DT), steam desolventization of oilseed meal
couples hexane removal, water uptake and heat transfer. We formulate a
conservative particle model containing free liquid hexane, binary pore
gas, retained water and hexane, and a separate external water film.
Component and energy jump conditions determine liquid-hexane interface
recession; after core extinction, the same inventories
evolve in a stationary radial particle. Published
thermodynamic and retention properties, together with declared transport
coefficients, parameterize the model. The
formulation is exercised in two baths with prescribed gas histories. In the first, a
water-bearing soybean particle in Faner's pure-hexane vapour at
\SI{120}{\celsius} falls from 0.20 to 0.10 kg hexane/kg dry meal in
\SI{\FanerFallingInterval}{\second}, compared with 25.4 s measured. The
source reports preheating, but not the exact initial temperature,
moisture or mass-weighted particle sizes. On a 64-cell radial mesh, the 1766.5-s DT history yields \DTFiniteMoisture\%
wet-basis moisture, \DTFiniteHexaneWet\ ppm wet-basis hexane and a mean temperature of
\SI{\DTFiniteTemperature}{\celsius}. In both calculations, independently accumulated
water, hexane and energy balances close within $10^{-10}$ in
normalized units through all three regimes. Mesh and time-step refinement
to 64 radial cells converges the Faner history and the DT outlet hexane
and temperature; DT outlet moisture converges more slowly, limited by a
thin surface diffusion layer.
Literature experiments and industrial outlet ranges benchmark the response
without fitting falling-rate trajectories or DT outlets. This common formulation
provides the particle-level basis for a DT digital twin that can shorten
process design and support model-based control.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} desolventization; soybean meal; n-hexane;
moving-front model; sorption; food process modeling

\paragraph*{Practical Applications.}
A prescribed-bath experiment lets a process engineer examine solvent
loss, moisture uptake and temperature without first solving a complete
desolventizer-toaster (DT). The same particle equations describe a
pure-hexane drying experiment and a steam-rich DT scenario. In the
pure-hexane case, the calculated particle dries faster than the measured
charge; better size, exposure and temperature information is needed to
explain the difference. In the DT case, the particle leaves with \DTFiniteMoisture\%
wet-basis moisture and \DTFiniteHexaneWet\ ppm hexane, inside the published
outlet ranges, with liquid-water imbibition included; the uptake rate
rests on one same-material measurement. Between the two finest meshes
these outlet values change by less than 1\% in hexane and by 0.07
percentage points in moisture. The model returns signed water,
hexane and energy fluxes that can later be coupled to conservative tray
balances.

\section*{Data availability}
The governing balances, parameters, comparison conventions and numerical
acceptance criteria are stated in the text, the tables and the submitted
Supplementary Material. The particle-model source, the case runner that
produced every history reported here, and the LaTeX sources are released at
\url{https://github.com/tomas3svoboda/dt_particle_model}
(snapshot \texttt{v1.1.0-submission}, with a SHA-256 manifest of every file).
All reported histories, including the mesh and time-step ladder of
Section~\ref{sec:verification:convergence}, were computed with one frozen
execution tree of the particle code (identifier 151). The released solver
files are byte-identical to that tree, apart from six documented path and
packaging patches, and its freeze manifest with the hash of every file is
included. Three data archives of the release hold the case records of
every ladder and saturated-loading case, the independent original-feed
audit of every history and the ladder report with its observed orders and
GCI estimates (\texttt{runtime151\_ladder\_records}), the complete histories
of the adopted 64-cell cases (\texttt{runtime151\_adopted\_cases}) and two
8-cell reference cases (\texttt{runtime151\_reference}); a release script reruns
the two reference cases and compares every recorded value with the
archived original (Sec.~\ref*{S-sup:deposit}). No preservation DOI has
yet been issued. Additional working notes are available from the
corresponding author on request.
Digitized readings of published figures are shared subject to the
publishers' redistribution terms, with reading half-widths and their sources.
The steady equipment-model calculation that supplies the prescribed bath
of Section~\ref{sec:validation:dtmarch} is not part of the release, but
its output is. Numerical material that does not yet exist is listed in
Section~\ref{sec:discussion:limits} and Sec.~\ref*{S-sup:missingfull};
it is not described as available.
