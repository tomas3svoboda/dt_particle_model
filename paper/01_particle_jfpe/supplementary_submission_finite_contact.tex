\section{Liquid-water imbibition: coefficient basis and numerical evidence}
\label{sup:finitecontact}
This section constructs the water-transport coefficients and reports
their sensitivities. The physical pathway, activation conditions, retained
inventory and omitted mechanisms are specified in main
Sec.~\ref*{M-sec:model:liquidcontact}.

The constitutive derivations below remain the basis of the updated DT case.
The factor, contact-conductance and earlier timestep tables retain their
original constant-property calculations: $D_b=6\times10^{-10}$ m$^2$/s
and oil capacity 2000 J/(kg K). They document earlier sensitivity evidence,
not sensitivity or convergence of the updated temperature-dependent case.
The updated nominal history and balances are reported in
Sec.~\ref{sup:dtmarch}; its property sensitivities are reported separately.

\paragraph{Reference transport coefficients.}
\label{sup:watermobility}
The current pore law and coefficient-convention limits accompany main
Eq.~\ref*{M-eq:porediffusivitytemperature}. No physical justification has
been established for the former scalar-to-binary factor of 1.5.

The reference retained-water mobility was originally derived
from the linearized coupled balances at $T=385.65$ K, $P=130$ kPa and
$y_{\mathrm h}=0.80$, with the reference retention properties.
Eliminating the total molar flux gives the local apparent response
\[
D_{\mathrm{app}}=D_{\mathrm{app,pore}}+g_{\mathrm{ret}}\mathcal L_{\mathrm{w,ret}},
\]
where $D_{\mathrm{app,pore}}=4.54\times10^{-12}$ m$^2$/s and
$g_{\mathrm{ret}}=7.26\times10^{-4}$ m$^3$/mol are obtained from the
same storage and chemical-potential derivatives. Setting the combined
response to $6.2\times10^{-10}$ m$^2$/s, with the former constant
$D_b=6.0\times10^{-10}$ m$^2$/s, gives the reference retained-water
mobility $8.47\times10^{-7}$ mol/(m s). The target value is the maximum apparent
moisture diffusivity reported by \citet{touffet2026moisture} for
pelleted feed at \SI{60}{\celsius}; transferring it to this reference
state is an engineering assumption. On the isothermal, uncapped Luikov
branch, the diffusivity of the retained-water path is
\[
D_{\mathrm{ret}}=
\frac{M_{\mathrm w}\mathcal L A_1}{\rho_{\mathrm{dm}}A_2W^2},
\]
which for the reference mobility is $3.8\times10^{-10}$ m$^2$/s at
$W=0.05$ and $2.4\times10^{-11}$ m$^2$/s at $W=0.20$. Compare this with
two oilseed-meal datasets: vapour-sorption diffusivities of
sunflower meal, $10^{-12}$ to $1.15\times10^{-10}$ m$^2$/s at
50--\SI{95}{\celsius} \citep{cardarelli1998thesis}, and the soaking
diffusivity of defatted soybean meal, $1.14$ to $2.07\times10^{-11}$
m$^2$/s at 20--\SI{60}{\celsius} \citep{mukherjee2019soaking}. The
reference retained-water mobility has a comparable scale to oilseed-meal
measurements, although its value was taken from the pellet study. It is
held fixed in the new full-history pore-diffusivity comparisons; the local
apparent response is not forced to remain exactly at its original target
when the pore law changes.

\paragraph{The liquid-water mobility factor.}
The selected total enhancement is $F=100$; the additional path is
$\mathcal L_\ell=(F-1)\mathcal L_{\mathrm{w,ret}}
=8.39\times10^{-5}$ mol/(m s). We estimate its scale from the hot-water
uptake of soybean meal reported by \citet{hemmingsen2008water}, using
the coarse mean sieve diameter 1.43 mm as an assumed sphere diameter.
The first coarse sample at \SI{80}{\celsius}, after 90 s immersion,
already has substantial uptake near the later cake-water level.
We interpret 90 s as an approximate equilibration timescale and use
95--99\% uptake as a declared endpoint convention, not source-measured
equilibration fractions. For constant diffusivity, uniform initial loading
and an equilibrated surface, the spherical solution \citep{crank1956diffusion}
requires Fourier numbers $Dt/R^2=0.253$--0.416 at these endpoints.
With $R=0.715$ mm and $t=90$ s this gives
$D(80\,^\circ\mathrm C)=(1.44$--$2.36)\times10^{-9}$ m$^2$/s.
An assumed inverse-viscosity transfer, using the water-viscosity ratio
$\mu(80\,^\circ\mathrm C)/\mu(100\,^\circ\mathrm C)=1.257$
\citep[eq.~7]{iapws2011liquidwater}, gives
$(1.81$--$2.97)\times10^{-9}$ m$^2$/s at \SI{100}{\celsius}.
Dividing by the baseline $D_{\mathrm{ret}}=2.377\times10^{-11}$ m$^2$/s
at the representative loading $W=0.20$ gives factors 76--125; we adopt
the rounded value 100. This selects a constant mobility enhancement from
an approximate transport scale; it is not a measured uncertainty interval
or an additional temperature-dependent liquid-transport law.

The source cake water includes external and interstitial water, and the
approximately 150-s drainage period does not isolate intraparticle uptake.
The sphere, 90-s particle-equilibration interpretation and viscosity
transfer are therefore engineering assumptions. The finite
$K_\ell=0.32$ mol/(m$^2$ s) is retained separately; the combined closure
is not fitted to reproduce the cake uptake in 90 s.
\citet{hong2006selfdiffusion} distinguish fast network water and slower
hydrated water in soft tofu, with molecular self-diffusivities
$2.26\times10^{-9}$ and $6.84\times10^{-11}$ m$^2$/s. This supports
distinct molecular mobilities, but self-diffusion is not the present
collective transport coefficient and does not set $F$.

The mobility and contact terms act in series. The following resistance
estimates and marches retain their earlier constant-property scope.
Table~\ref{tab:finitecontact:factor}
gives the interior share of the series resistance at the DT radius with
$d=\Rp/5$, the lumped-sphere equivalent, for $K_\ell=0.32$ mol/(m$^2$ s).
Above a factor of about 300 the surface carries most of this lumped
resistance. At 100 the interior approximately doubles it; a larger
contact conductance can partly compensate. These resistance estimates
do not identify the two coefficients from the cake-water data.
Completed DT marches at factors
100, 1000 and 10\,000 with the same $K_\ell$
(Table~\ref{tab:finitecontact:factormarch}) place the outlet on a plateau
between 1000 and 10\,000; where between 100 and 1000 the plateau begins
was not marched. The tenfold
reduction to 100 lowers the outlet moisture by 0.58 percentage points
and raises hexane by 22.8 mg/kg dry; the tenfold increase to
10\,000 moves them by only $+0.07$ points and $-2.7$ mg/kg dry. The earlier
constant-property outlet is on its plateau at 1000; the adopted factor
100 includes appreciable internal resistance.

\paragraph{Construction of the contact conductance.}
\label{sup:contactscale}
The liquid contact conductance represents the resistance to liquid entry
between the external water film and the connected particle matrix. It keeps
the surface conductance finite when the shell forms, where the main-text law
gives $G_\ell\to K_\ell$ as $d\to0$. A conditional rate scale is constructed
from hot-water uptake by soybean meal
\citep{hemmingsen2008water}: the coarse sieve fraction (above 0.5 mm,
mean 1.43 mm; 25 g fat per kg dry matter; initial water 11.8\%, that is
$W_0=0.134$ kg/kg dry) immersed at about \SI{80}{\celsius}. Its
rehydration index is near its later level at the first sample, 90 s,
with some subsequent change. The construction assumes a sphere of radius
$R=0.715$ mm, entry through the surface alone, the surface at the film's state
($W_{\mathrm{cap}}$) and the retention law of the main text. The water
balance $(\rho_{\mathrm{dm}}V/M_{\mathrm w})\dot W
=-A K_\ell[\psi(W)-\psi(W_{\mathrm{cap}})]$ with
$\psi(W)=(1-A_1/W)/A_2$ integrates in closed form to
\[
t=\frac{\rho_{\mathrm{dm}}R A_2}{3M_{\mathrm w}A_1K_\ell}\,
W_{\mathrm{cap}}\!\left[(W_0-W_1)+W_{\mathrm{cap}}
\ln\frac{W_{\mathrm{cap}}-W_0}{W_{\mathrm{cap}}-W_1}\right],
\]
and requiring $W_1=W_{\mathrm{cap}}-0.01$ kg/kg at $t=90$ s gives
$K_\ell=0.253$ mol/(m$^2$ s) at \SI{80}{\celsius}. Scaling the entry
rate with the inverse viscosity of water from 80 to \SI{100}{\celsius}
(a factor of 1.26) gives 0.318, adopted as 0.32. Changing the assumed
end-point tolerance to 0.02 or 0.005
kg/kg below capacity gives 0.21 or 0.43. Reading the measurement at
\SI{20}{\celsius} with the same construction and viscosity scaling
(a factor of 3.55) gives 0.90, so the adopted value is the lower,
less extrapolated, of the two readings.

The mapping assumes a spherical sieve diameter, the chosen retained-water
endpoint and inverse-viscosity scaling. The source measures wet filter-cake
mass per dry-solid mass after about 150 s of vacuum drainage. Its water
includes particle, interstitial and surface contributions, which are not
separated; the cake uptake also exceeds the model's retained capacity.
Thus 90 s is not an isolated intraparticle equilibration time or a strict
bound on it. The resulting $K_\ell$ is a conditional scale estimate,
transferred to DT meal without fitting its outlet.

With the adopted liquid-water mobility, the resistance length
$\mathcal L_\ell/K_\ell$ is 0.262 mm, 0.175 times the DT radius. At
$d=\Rp$ the series conductance is $G_\ell=0.0476$ mol/(m$^2$ s), and the
contact term's share of the series resistance is 0.149. The contact-only time
$\tau_K=\rho_{\mathrm{dm}}\Rp A_2W^2/(3M_{\mathrm w}K_\ell A_1)$ gives
56 s at $W=0.20$, and the closed-form fill of a DT sphere limited by contact alone,
from the feed loading 0.124 to $W_{\mathrm{cap}}-0.01$, takes 153 s
(491 s in the earlier reference case 0.10). These are scale estimates;
radial gradients, pore-gas exchange, the changing surface state, the
film's water stock and the capacity all remain active in the calculation.

\paragraph{Sensitivity to the contact conductance.}
\label{sup:contactsensitivity}
Table~\ref{tab:finitecontact:sensitivity} lists completed DT marches at
four liquid contact conductances with every other input held constant,
the earlier reference case 0.10 among them. The outlet moisture is bounded by the condensate supply
and the retention capacity: the external water film is consumed before
the exit in every case, and a fourfold change in $K_\ell$ moves the outlet
moisture by 1.3 percentage points and the wet-basis hexane by 43 ppm.
The conductance sets how long the film persists, not how much water the
particle finally holds.

\paragraph{Earlier constant-property events and refinement.}
The earlier DT calculation uses the feed history up to 492.257073 s,
effective radius 1.5 mm, two radial cells, the prescribed bath and the
existing retention capacity. The moving-front calculation begins with a
0.01-s shell-formation interval, advances regime B in 0.1-s intervals,
shortened where events require, and resolves grid-face arrival and departure
before core extinction at 503.560810 s. External water stays positive
throughout regime B, so the zero-film regime-B branch, which is not
implemented, is not exercised (Sec.~\ref{sup:dtmarch}).
Regime C advances the stationary particle and the film together to the
film's depletion at 624.548745 s and then to the exit.
Table~\ref{tab:finitecontact:refinement} gives the regime-C step
refinement, performed in the earlier reference case $K_\ell=0.10$ from the
same moving-front end point; the 5-s maximum step is used in every
reported march. The extra digits resolve the numerical sensitivity; the main
text uses rounded values.

\paragraph{Earlier constant-property independent balances.}
An independent reconstruction of the earlier reference march, from dry-shell
formation to exit, evaluates water (W), hexane (H) and energy (U) in the
particle and external water film at every accepted step. The maximum
normalized W/H/U defects are $4.24\times10^{-11}$, $8.61\times10^{-13}$ and $6.16\times10^{-12}$,
below the $10^{-10}$ acceptance limit; the regime-B segment alone gives
$1.05\times10^{-11}$, $8.61\times10^{-13}$ and $3.94\times10^{-12}$. The
same reconstruction stays below $4.2\times10^{-11}$ for the other three
conductances of Table~\ref{tab:finitecontact:sensitivity}. The
unchanged feed history keeps its earlier independent check, which stays
below $3.3\times10^{-13}$ up to dry-shell formation. Component partial
enthalpies count each energy transfer once. These checks quantify component and energy conservation in the reported
particle calculation. Complete-history mesh and time-step refinement
(Sec.~\ref{sup:dtmarch}) and independent identification of the material
coefficients provide complementary evidence about accuracy and physical
prediction.
