\subsection{Verification of balances and numerical operators}
\label{sec:verification}
\label{sec:verification:table}
\label{sec:verification:notvalidation}
\label{sec:verification:donor}

The operator benchmarks retain their declared constant-property reference
configurations. The temperature-dependent property extension is checked
separately by the energy derivative and caloric inversion identities, the
wet and dry heat capacities, binary face-flux scaling and retained-water
invariance; the two reported histories use the same conservation gates.

Manufactured solutions give spatial orders $1.95$--$2.00$ and temporal
orders $0.97$--$1.00$ for the fixed-domain components; the moving-front
time operator gives $0.98$--$0.99$. Every step of the short refinement ladder satisfies
component and energy balances at
or below $10^{-10}$ in physically normalized units (at most
$7.8\times10^{-13}$ on that ladder). The same swept volume in all balances
enforces the discrete geometric conservation identity exactly. Shifting the
arbitrary enthalpy zero of hexane does not change the solution: the observed
datum-invariance difference is $3.42\times10^{-10}$ under its separately
stated environment allowance. The discrete identity
\eqref{eq:gauge} is the corresponding test for water.
The operator table, an illustrative sensitivity with an inadmissible
limiting-case initial state, and unresolved tests are in Sec.~\ref*{S-sup:verification}.

The datum test exposed an earlier mismatch between equilibrium-trace and
bulk-state debits of swept material. The corrected component and energy
jumps use the same wet-side material state
(Eqs.~\eqref{eq:rhcomp}--\eqref{eq:rhenergy}). Conservation does not identify
transport coefficients or establish plant accuracy. Front refinement,
including a nonconverged flux-integral result, is reported in
Sec.~\ref*{S-sup:acceptance}.

\subsection{Convergence of the two particle histories}
\label{sec:verification:convergence}
Operator orders do not show that a complete history, with its moving
front, regime changes and surface events, converges as the mesh and time
step are refined. Both experiments were therefore recomputed from their
original feeds with one frozen version of the code, at 8, 16, 32 and
64 uniform radial cells and at three time levels, each halving every
step bound of the one before. The results were scored against accuracy
goals declared before the runs: for mesh refinement, 1\% in outlet hexane,
0.02 percentage points in outlet moisture and 0.1~K in outlet temperature;
for time refinement, 0.2\%, 0.005 percentage points and 0.02~K. Observed
orders, Richardson extrapolation and the grid convergence index (GCI,
safety factor 1.25) are used only where successive differences decrease
consistently \citep{roache1998verification}; tables and history-wide
measures are in Sec.~\ref*{S-sup:ladder}. All completed histories pass the
independent whole-history conservation audit at $10^{-10}$.

The Faner history is converged. It refines at second order in the radial
mesh (observed orders 1.8 to 2.1 for outlet moisture and temperature) and
at first order in time, and meets every goal but one: between the two
finest time levels its outlet temperature changes by 0.018~K, inside the
declared 0.02~K, but the GCI estimate with its safety factor is 0.022~K.
The computed falling interval is 17.7~s at every resolution from 4 to
64 cells and at every time level, so its difference from the measured
25.4~s does not come from the numerics (Section~\ref{sec:discussion}).
At 64 cells only the two coarser time levels exist, because the
shell-birth search at the finest level reaches the edge of the declared
$|\beta|\le3$ study band of the film law; Faner's time convergence is read
at 8 to 32 cells.

The DT history converges in time at about first order at every mesh; at
64 cells the two finest time levels differ by 0.07\% in outlet hexane,
0.0008 percentage points in moisture and 0.0007~K. In the mesh, outlet
hexane changes by 3.3\%, 1.4\% and 0.57\% over the three refinements
(observed order 1.3) and outlet temperature by 0.031~K at the last one;
both meet their goals at 64 cells. Outlet moisture converges more slowly,
by 0.112, 0.094 and 0.066 percentage points (order 0.5), and does not
meet the 0.02-point goal. The cause is the surface condition of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:filmfreesurface}: the retained loading jumps from the
surface value to the outer-cell value across a diffusion layer that is
thinner than the 64-cell half-cell, \SI{11.7}{\micro\metre}, until about
600~s and is about 12 and \SI{21}{\micro\metre} thick at 700 and 900~s.
The deeper region of water above $\Wcap$, about \SI{200}{\micro\metre}
thick, is converged. A radial mesh graded towards the surface is the next
refinement. The GCI estimate of the remaining 64-cell outlet moisture error,
0.19 percentage points, is smaller than the 0.76-point effect of
$W_{\mathrm{sat}}$ and small against the 16--22\% industrial outlet range.
Along the whole DT history the largest mesh differences, 5.3\% in hexane
and 0.15 percentage points in moisture between 32 and 64 cells, exceed
their goals; the hexane difference peaks in the short front stage and the
moisture difference in the film-free period, so the outlet values are
better resolved than the transient between them. The former production resolutions,
two cells for DT and four for Faner, differed from 64 cells by 12.0\% in
outlet hexane and 0.66 percentage points in outlet moisture for DT, and by
0.011\% in outlet hexane for Faner. The results of
Section~\ref{sec:validation} use 64 cells at the middle time level.
