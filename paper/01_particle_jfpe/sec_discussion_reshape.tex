\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}\label{sec:discussion:scope}
\label{sec:discussion:coefficients}\label{sec:discussion:refusal}\label{sec:discussion:envelope}
The calculated Faner particle passes from 0.20 to 0.10 kg/kg in a shorter
interval than the measured charge. The measured interval is a property of
a charge, not of one particle: the source reports only summaries of a
broad size distribution, and its holder carries a layer three to five
particle diameters deep, in which some particles still hold a liquid-hexane
core while others are already in the falling-rate period. Both effects
spread the charge-average loading over a longer interval, and a single
declared sphere carries neither. Because the calculated interval, 17.7~s,
and the temperature at 235~s are converged
(Section~\ref{sec:verification:convergence}), the gap to the measured
25.4~s is attributed to this difference between one sphere and a dispersed
charge, not to the numerics; the temperature gap carries
the same dispersity together with the difference between a local holder
thermocouple and a particle-mean temperature. The setup
uncertainties collected in Sec.~\ref*{S-sup:fanerinputs} prevent a finer
apportionment. Larger particles can prolong
removal even when small particles heat earlier, but their contribution
requires mass weighting and completed class histories
(Sec.~\ref*{S-sup:fanersizes}). Likewise, a subcooled start requires a new
initial-value calculation with sensible heating, rather than a time shift
of the temperature curve. Simultaneous loading, spatial temperature and
size-resolved observations would distinguish startup, heat exposure and
internal transport effects.

DT residence and heat supply must accommodate external-liquid removal,
core recession and the retained-solvent tail \citep{kemper2013solvent}.
Moist heat must also reduce antinutritional activity while preserving
protein quality \citep{witte_nd_soybeanmeal,mustakas1981critical}; no
quality kinetics are modelled here. Condensation supplies heat but retained
water increases downstream drying duty \citep{witte_nd_soybeanmeal}.
Quantifying steam savings therefore requires steam, indirect-heat and
dryer balances beyond these prescribed-bath calculations.

Two independent equilibrium checks give further context for DT practice.
At gas compositions selected from last-tray literature, the dry-shell hexane equilibrium floor is
4.5--66.0 ppm without the separate oil-solution sensitivity and
8.1--112.4 ppm with it (Sec.~\ref*{S-sup:desolventizerfloor}). These
equilibrium quantities do not set the residence needed to reach them.
For retained water, a separate ideal-vapour diagnostic defines $T^*$ by
\begin{equation}
\aw(\Wret_{\mathrm w})P_{\mathrm{sat}}^{\mathrm w}(T^*)=P.
\label{eq:sorbedbp}
\end{equation}
Here $\Wret_{\mathrm w}$ is retained-water loading, excluding the external
water film. This water-only diagnostic equates approximate equilibrium water-vapour
pressure to total pressure; the binary calculation instead matches water
fugacity to the gas water component. It neither fixes transient temperature
nor establishes DT boiling.
Applying the earlier supplied-moisture diagnostic under the assumption
that this moisture is retained gives 100.5--\SI{107.6}{\celsius}.
It combines observations from different sources; at the moisture of the
temperature study, the diagnostic temperature exceeds the observation
by 2.1 K (Sec.~\ref*{S-sup:envelopescoring}).

A separate free-water droplet has unit water activity and, neglecting
capillary-pressure effects, boils near \SI{100}{\celsius} at
\SI{101.3}{\kilo\pascal} \citep{wagner2002iapws,iapws2018r695}.
Its boiling temperature cannot be raised by assigning the lower activity
of retained water elsewhere in the particle. Our retained-water inventory
does not resolve molecularly bound and interstitial water, so bulk
moisture alone cannot establish that all water has the isotherm activity.
The 100.5--\SI{107.6}{\celsius} interval is therefore a conditional
equilibrium diagnostic, not evidence of a measured boiling-point elevation
of free liquid in meal. Neither this diagnostic nor agreement with
industrial outlet ranges validates the coupled transient particle or the
industrial tower.

The water film's assumed transparency to hexane may materially affect
regime-C removal. The imbibition assumptions in
Section~\ref{sec:model:liquidcontact} also require independent evidence. Transient uptake
measurements on extracted meal, with external liquid quantified separately
and several particle sizes, are needed to distinguish contact and interior
resistances. An outlet moisture match alone cannot identify them.

\label{sec:discussion:extrapolation}\label{sec:discussion:geometry}\label{sec:discussion:limits}
Material and numerical limits set the present scope. The retention
extensions and fluid-property domains are stated with their closures in
Section~\ref{sec:thermo:properties}; the effective pore reference and
fixed-hindrance assumption accompany Eq.~\eqref{eq:porediffusivitytemperature}.
These transport inputs remain to be identified from paired water--hexane
measurements. Fixed geometry, an immobile connected core and the isobaric
total-flux closure restrict the represented mechanisms
(Sections~\ref{sec:physical:regimes}--\ref{sec:model:front}).
On uniform meshes of up to 64 cells at three time levels, the reported
Faner values and the DT outlet hexane and temperature are converged
(Section~\ref{sec:verification:convergence}). The DT outlet moisture still
changes by 0.066 percentage points over the last mesh refinement, at an
order of about one half, because a thin diffusion layer under the
film-free surface is not yet resolved; a radial mesh graded towards the
surface is the next refinement. That remaining moisture uncertainty is small against the
16--22\% industrial outlet range and smaller than the physical
sensitivities: over the prescribed pore-diffusivity range the outlet
moisture moves by 0.41 percentage points and the hexane by about
$\pm2$\% (Section~\ref{sec:validation:dtmarch}), and over the published
range of $W_{\mathrm{sat}}$ by 0.76 points and $-33$\% to $+11$\%
(Section~\ref{sec:thermo:properties}).

\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusions}\label{sec:discussion:measurements}
One conservative formulation completes a 240-s pure-hexane experiment
and a 1766.5-s prescribed DT history through external-liquid depletion,
core recession and extinction, with separately conserved condensate.
Both use measurement-based soybean-oil heat capacity with its consistent
energy integral and a Fuller-shaped pore diffusivity. The effective pore
reference and fixed hindrance remain unvalidated for solvent-wet meal.

The declared Faner particle falls from 0.20 to 0.10 kg hexane/kg dry meal
in \SI{\FanerFallingInterval}{\second}, compared with 25.4 s measured. The DT calculation includes liquid-water imbibition
and reaches \DTFiniteMoisture\% wet-basis moisture, \DTFiniteHexaneWet\ ppm wet-basis hexane and a
volume-mean particle temperature of \SI{\DTFiniteTemperature}{\celsius}. Its liquid
contact conductance is a conditional scale estimate from same-material
uptake and its internal mobility is estimated from the soybean-meal
size and uptake timescale. These transferred estimates are not jointly
identified from DT-meal measurements.

Refinement of both complete histories to 64 radial cells and three time
levels shows that these results belong to the model equations rather than
to the discretization. The Faner history converges at second order in
mesh and first order in time, and its falling interval is the same at
every resolution. The DT outlet hexane and temperature converge within
the declared 1\% and 0.1~K; the DT outlet moisture converges at order one
half under a thin surface diffusion layer, to within about 0.2 percentage
points, and a surface-graded mesh is the next refinement. The DT outlet
hexane depends more on the declared saturated retained-water loading than
on the numerics, which makes that loading a priority for measurement.

\begin{samepage}
The gas-phase transport mechanism differs between the two computed
regime-C histories. In the pure-hexane experiment, total vapour motion
carries hexane outward and binary diffusion contributes a small opposing
flux. In the steam-rich DT history, while the particle takes up water,
binary diffusion carries hexane outward against inward total vapour
motion; once the particle begins to dry, outward vapour motion carries it.
The same phase regime thus admits different flux pathways; their relative
magnitudes do not identify the rate-limiting resistance.
\par
\end{samepage}

The model's signed water, hexane and energy fluxes can be coupled to tray
balances for DT design; in that coupled form the formulation is the
particle-level basis for a DT digital twin that can shorten process
design and support model-based control. The results connect the model's
mechanisms to observable trajectories and show which independent
geometry, uptake and temperature measurements are needed before it is
used for predictive equipment calculations.
