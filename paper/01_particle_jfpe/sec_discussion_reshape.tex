\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}\label{sec:discussion:scope}
\label{sec:discussion:coefficients}\label{sec:discussion:refusal}\label{sec:discussion:envelope}
The two prescribed-bath experiments show how a common particle
formulation connects external-liquid evaporation, internal-core recession
and residual-component transport. Their signed component and energy
exchanges can supply a future tray calculation. Prescribing the bath
isolates this particle response, while leaving countercurrent vapour,
wall, residence and discharge balances to the equipment model. We first
interpret the Faner comparison, then its meaning for DT practice and what
is needed next.

The calculated Faner particle dries faster than the measured charge.
The setup uncertainties collected in Sec.~\ref*{S-sup:fanerinputs} prevent
assigning that discrepancy to one mechanism. Larger particles can prolong
removal even when small particles heat earlier, but their contribution
requires mass weighting and completed class histories
(Sec.~\ref*{S-sup:fanersizes}). Likewise, a subcooled start requires a new
initial-value calculation with sensible heating, rather than a time shift
of the temperature curve. Simultaneous loading, spatial temperature and
size-resolved observations would distinguish startup, heat exposure and
internal transport effects.

For DT practice, the three regimes divide the heat demand.
Indirect heating in PD removes external free hexane before live-steam
contact; steam condensation then supplies heat for rapid solvent removal,
and the remaining retained hexane determines the later approach to the
outlet residual. Industrial DT residence and heat supply must serve these
successive needs \citep{kemper2013solvent}. At the same time, the moist heat
treatment must reduce antinutritional activity while preserving useful
protein quality \citep{witte_nd_soybeanmeal,mustakas1981critical}.
The calculated temperature and moisture histories describe that exposure;
a meal-quality kinetic model is outside the present scope.

The moisture result also matters for energy design: water retained
at DT exit contributes to the load on the downstream dryer, which takes
meal towards its storage moisture \citep{witte_nd_soybeanmeal}.
More condensation can accelerate heat supply and solvent removal, while
more retained water can increase later drying duty. A steam-saving claim
therefore requires the full balance of steam, indirect heat and drying
energy, rather than outlet solvent or moisture alone. These prescribed-bath
experiments expose that coupling without quantifying a plant steam saving.

Two independent equilibrium checks give further context for DT practice.
At literature-motivated last-tray compositions, the dry-shell hexane equilibrium floor is
4.5--66.0 ppm without the separate oil-solution sensitivity and
8.1--112.4 ppm with it (Sec.~\ref*{S-sup:desolventizerfloor}). These
equilibrium quantities do not set the residence needed to reach them.
For retained water, a separate ideal-vapour diagnostic defines $T^*$ by
\begin{equation}
\aw(\Wret_{\mathrm w})P_{\mathrm{sat}}^{\mathrm w}(T^*)=P.
\label{eq:sorbedbp}
\end{equation}
Here $\Wret_{\mathrm w}$ is retained-water loading, excluding the external
water film. The product on the left is its approximate equilibrium water
vapour pressure. Equating it to total pressure is a water-only diagnostic;
the actual binary particle calculation instead matches water fugacity
to the water component of the gas. Equation~\eqref{eq:sorbedbp} neither
pins the transient particle temperature nor establishes boiling in the DT.
Applying the earlier supplied-moisture diagnostic under the assumption
that this moisture is retained gives 100.5--\SI{107.6}{\celsius}.
It combines observations from different sources; at the moisture of the
temperature study, the diagnostic temperature exceeds the observation
by 2.1 K (Sec.~\ref*{S-sup:envelopescoring}).

A separate free-water droplet has unit water activity and, neglecting
capillary-pressure effects, boils near \SI{100}{\celsius} at
\SI{101.3}{\kilo\pascal} \citep{wagner2002iapws,iapws2018r695}.
Its boiling temperature cannot be raised by assigning the lower activity
of retained water elsewhere in the particle. Our retained-water inventory
does not resolve molecularly bound and interstitial water, so bulk
moisture alone cannot establish that all water has the isotherm activity.
The 100.5--\SI{107.6}{\celsius} interval is therefore a conditional
equilibrium diagnostic, not evidence of a measured boiling-point elevation
of free liquid in meal. Neither this diagnostic nor agreement with
industrial outlet ranges validates the coupled transient particle or the
industrial tower.

Hexane leaves retained storage, enters the pore gas through local
equilibrium and is carried outward by binary diffusion and total vapour
motion. Retained hexane has no separate radial liquid flux; water also
moves through the matrix (Section~\ref{sec:model:local}). These paths
continue after core extinction without a fitted particle-mean tail law.
For DT, the additional imbibition pathway and its material
transfer assumptions are specified in Section~\ref{sec:model:liquidcontact}.
Because the water film persists into regime C, its assumed transparency
to hexane may materially affect solvent removal. Transient uptake
measurements on extracted meal, with external liquid quantified separately
and several particle sizes, are needed to distinguish contact and interior
resistances. An outlet moisture match alone cannot identify them.

\label{sec:discussion:extrapolation}\label{sec:discussion:geometry}\label{sec:discussion:limits}
Material and numerical limits set the present scope. The retention
extensions and fluid-property domains are stated with their closures in
Section~\ref{sec:thermo:properties}; the effective pore reference and
fixed-hindrance assumption accompany Eq.~\eqref{eq:porediffusivitytemperature}.
These transport inputs remain to be identified from paired water--hexane
measurements. Fixed geometry, an immobile connected core and the isobaric
total-flux closure restrict the represented mechanisms
(Sections~\ref{sec:physical:regimes}--\ref{sec:model:front}).
The two-cell DT and four-cell Faner histories are complete discrete
experiments; operator verification and selected timestep checks do not yet
establish whole-history continuum convergence.

\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusions}\label{sec:discussion:measurements}
One conservative particle formulation completes both a 240-s
water-bearing particle experiment in pure superheated hexane and a
1766.5-s prescribed DT history. Component and energy balances
continue through external-liquid depletion, internal-front recession and
core extinction. External condensate is stored separately and exchanges
water and energy with the particle and the prescribed gas.
Both histories use temperature-dependent soybean-oil heat capacity with
its consistent energy integral and a Fuller-shaped binary pore diffusivity.
The oil correlation is measurement-based; the effective pore reference
and its assumed fixed hindrance remain to be validated for solvent-wet meal.

The declared Faner particle falls from 0.20 to 0.10 kg hexane/kg dry meal
in \SI{\FanerFallingInterval}{\second}, compared with 25.4 s measured. The DT calculation includes liquid-water imbibition
and reaches \DTFiniteMoisture\% wet-basis moisture, \DTFiniteHexaneWet\ ppm wet-basis hexane and a
volume-mean particle temperature of \SI{\DTFiniteTemperature}{\celsius}. Its liquid
contact conductance is a conditional scale estimate from same-material
uptake and its internal mobility is estimated from the soybean-meal
size and uptake timescale. These transferred estimates are not jointly
identified from DT-meal measurements.

\begin{samepage}
The gas-phase transport mechanism differs between the two computed
regime-C histories. In the pure-hexane experiment, total vapour motion
carries hexane outward and binary diffusion contributes a small opposing
flux. In the late steam-rich DT history, binary diffusion carries hexane
outward against inward total vapour motion. The phase regime alone
therefore does not determine which gas-phase contribution carries solvent
removal. These findings identify the flux pathways; a dominant flux
contribution does not by itself identify the rate-limiting resistance.
\par
\end{samepage}

The model's signed water, hexane and energy fluxes can be coupled to tray
balances for DT design; in that coupled form the formulation is the
particle-level basis for a DT digital twin that can shorten process
design and support model-based control. The results connect the model's
mechanisms to observable trajectories and show which independent
geometry, uptake and temperature measurements are needed before it is
used for predictive equipment calculations.
