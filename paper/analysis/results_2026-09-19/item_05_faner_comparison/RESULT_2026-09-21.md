# Item 5, 2026-09-21: this supersedes `RESULT.md` — the Faner march repaired on physical grounds

`RESULT.md` (the 2026-09-20 pass) and the whole `rerun_2026-09-20/` folder are
kept byte-identical beside this file. Five changes were made, each derived from
a published source or from the source's own tables, none fitted to a Faner
curve, and each with its extrapolations and conventions declared. Two of them
make a number in `RESULT.md` **worse**, and that is reported here rather than
avoided.

**A. Stage 1 on the geometry of the experiment — the largest single
improvement.** The transfer area is now the printed sample-holder footprint
(0.011 m²) over the sample's own dry mass, from the source's printed sample mass
and initial loading: 0.6377, 0.5337, 0.5466 and 0.5497 m² per kg dry meal. The
frozen Coletto pair is unchanged and its declared 0.5/1.0/2.0 interval is kept.
The constant-rate demand goes from **7.0–8.1 times over-predicted to 1.33–1.53**
at the nominal film, and **the declared film interval now contains the
measurement at all four conditions, where before it contained it at none**
(0.67–3.06 against a measured 1.0). The stage-1 duration lands at 0.58–0.68 of
the measured `X_c` crossing, bracketed by the interval (0.58 to 1.37). *This is
not the same arithmetic as dividing the 2026-09-20 per-particle prediction by
that record's area ratio of 7.82–9.71, and the disclosure travels with it: that
ratio was built from the SOURCE's particle area on the TOTAL sample mass, while
the marched per-particle area is the FROZEN one per kg DRY mass, and the two
differ by 1.49–1.77 — exactly the gap between the two records' numbers.*

**B. A measured diffusivity law.** Cardarelli's 34 tabulated set-of-spheres
coefficients converted to this model's storage-consistent definition by the
measured identity `D* = r D_table`, with `r` measured at every point (1.19 to
18.98) and shown invariant to `D` to 3.7e-9 relative. Regression
`ln D* = ln A − (Ea/Rg)/T + β ln(X/0.01)`: soybean `A` = 3.513e-9 m²/s,
`Ea` = **0.31 ± 16.09 kJ/mol**, `β` = 0.959 ± 0.331, `σ(ln D*)` = 0.410 on 14
degrees of freedom. **Once the storage capacity is carried explicitly the pore
diffusivity is indistinguishable from athermal and close to proportional to
loading**; the activation energies the source reports are largely the
temperature dependence of the sorption capacity. Two extrapolations are
declared, and the loading one is the larger: 0.10/0.46/0.72 of the measured
`1/T` span beyond its hot end at 100/120/136 °C, but a **factor 6.4 to 12.8
beyond the largest loading ever measured** (1.56e-2 kg/kg) inside Faner's
falling-rate window, which the measured set does not overlap at all.

**B, the construction limit.** The certified dry shell takes one scalar `D_eff`

298.9 s to the last measured loading (2008 soybean). **The law is therefore
reported and drawn as a bracket spanning nearly two decades, never as a line**,
and `D(X(r))` is now a named refused element.

For a diffusivity that increases with loading, desorption from a sphere is
governed by the region of lowest diffusivity, which is the surface, and the
surface-evaluated coefficient is the scalar the space-resolved solution
approaches (Crank, *The Mathematics of Diffusion*, 1956); the volume-mean
evaluation over-predicts the flux by the ratio of the two coefficients, about
a decade here. The surface edge is therefore the physically indicated one,
and it favours the thesis; the volume-mean edge, which favours the journal,
is the unphysical end of the bracket. Both are reported; neither is selected.
